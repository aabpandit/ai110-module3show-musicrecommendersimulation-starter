# 🎧 Model Card: Music Recommender Simulation

## 1. Model Name  

Musical_Matchmaker

---

## 2. Intended Use  

Musical_Matchmaker is a classroom experiment for recommending songs (from a limited dataset) that matches a user's profile. The profile has to specify the attributes such as enegry level, genre, mood, etc that matches the user's profile.

---

## 3. How the Model Works  

Explain your scoring approach in simple language.  

Prompts:  

A song's attributes such as genre, mood, energy, etc are captured in the data. The UserProfile stores variables like favGenre, favMood, target_energy (a number between 0 and 1 representing intense/gentle music preferance), and a likesAcoustic (instrumental/synthesized sounds preference). Importantly, the profile stores a target — not a minimum or maximum — which means the system rewards songs that are closest to what the user described, not songs that simply score highest on any individual feature.

Once this UserProfile is created, each song is evaluated by computing four separate sub-scores and combining them into one final number. Those four sub-scores are then multiplied by weights that reflect their importance: genre carries 35%, energy 30%, mood 20%, and danceability 15%, summing to a final score between 0.0 and 1.0. Then the songs are ranked by their score and the top k results are returned as recommendations. 

---

## 4. Data  

The dataset the model is generated by Codepath and Claude. There are 20 songs with a mix of genres including pop, lofi, electronic, etc. The dataset is not very balaneced currently.

---

## 5. Strengths  

The system performs best when a user's preferred genre is well-represented in the catalog and their target energy is distinctive. The system is also very adaptive to changes weights and seeing the change.
---

## 6. Limitations and Bias 

The model treated genre match as a binary classifier. So it remained fixated on matching the genre or not matching at all. This prevented it from recognizing similar genres, also because it was weigned similarly to energy. From the output: The EDM Listener's top 3 were Pulse Protocol (electronic), Neon Bloom (k-pop), and Gym Hero (pop). Night Drive Loop, a synthwave song, is the most electronically adjacent genre in the catalog but scored only 0.53 and didn't make the top 3, ranked below k-pop purely because Neon Bloom shared the euphoric mood.

---

## 7. Evaluation  

Three user profiles were tested — Hip-Hop Fan, Acoustic Low-Energy Listener, and High-Tempo EDM Listener. For each profile, the primary check was whether the top-ranked song matched the profile's exact genre and mood, and whether scores dropped logically as those features diverged. All three profiles returned a perfect 1.00 for their intended match, confirming the scoring formula worked correctly. The most revealing test was changing the weights — halving genre and doubling energy.
